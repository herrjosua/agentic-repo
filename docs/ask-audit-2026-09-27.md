# Corpus Consistency Audit — 2026-09-27

Audit of the research corpus for cross-record contradictions, using the
real `POST /api/ask` endpoint (gemma2:9b + nomic-embed-text, running
locally) rather than a manual read-through. Ties to the v0.5.25 ticket
following the RAG endpoint's launch, which surfaced a real contradiction
(onboarding step 3) during live use.

This file is not fed to `/api/ask` — it lives in `docs/`, which the
endpoint doesn't index — so its quotes of old, now-corrected wording
won't confuse retrieval going forward.

## Method

51 questions sent to `/api/ask` in three rounds:

- **Round 1** (25 broad questions): onboarding, ambient scribe, session
  timeout/MFA, prior auth, burnout and nursing surveys, the patient
  chatbot, alert triage, AI governance and PHI, billing codes,
  accessibility, rollout, scope exclusions.
- **Round 2** (15 targeted questions): aimed at the suspects round 1
  turned up, plus a direct check of every count and duration in the
  findings against the raw session notes.
- **Round 3** (11 spot-checks): re-asked each fixed topic after edits,
  to confirm the fix actually resolved the contradiction in a live
  answer.

## Fixed

### Onboarding step 3 (the known bug)

The records weren't describing two different wizards — they were
counting steps two different ways:

- **Counting Signup as step 1**: the user-flow doc, the research plan
  ("6-step wizard"), both evaluations, the finding, the readout, the
  funnel summary, and raw session 1. This gives step 3 = Connect
  calendar.
- **Counting from the step after Signup**: the topline summary and the
  downstream design deliverables. This gives step 3 = Invite your team.

The raw notes themselves use both conventions — session 2 calls the
calendar step "step 2", and session 3's "step 3" worry is actually
about emailing the team.

**Canonical version chosen** (most independent support): 1 Signup,
2 Basics, 3 Connect calendar, 4 Invite your team, 5 Preferences,
6 Review & finish. Renumbered 9 deliverables to match; added a
step-numbering note to the finding.

**Root cause found in round 2**: markdown list numbers are stripped
when a record is chunked into passages for retrieval, so the model was
recounting the steps itself from prose and dropping Signup. Added
explicit "Step N —" text into `user-flows/onboarding-flow.md` to stop
this. (Flagged separately as a CRUD UI-side retrieval issue — likely
affects every other numbered list in the corpus, not just this one.)

**Evidence overstatement also found and corrected**: the finding and
readout both claimed "all 3 participants paused at step 3." The raw
notes don't support this — 2 of 3 tried to skip the calendar step, and
session 3's hesitation was specifically about the invite step, not the
calendar step. Corrected in both files.

### Other contradictions fixed

In each case, resolved by following the raw notes or the record other
files cite as their source:

- **Pulse survey baseline** — the rollout finding compared results to
  "the September survey"; the raw notes show it was February's nursing
  survey. Corrected.
- **Governance session count** — "four executive-level sessions, plus…"
  implied six sessions total. There are four sessions total, two of
  them executive-level. Corrected.
- **Longitudinal persona** — described P09 as "the one participant"
  across all three rounds. The v0.2 raw notes confirm three returning
  participants; P09 is one of three. Corrected.
- **Adoption topline title** — titled "Week 1," and the model repeated
  that framing in answers. The raw review it summarizes covers the
  first 4 weeks. Retitled "Early Adoption" (kept the existing filename
  to avoid breaking links).
- **v4.1 status indicator** — the doc claimed it replaced three
  instances; the screening it cites found two components. "Three"
  elsewhere in the corpus refers to three audited surfaces, not three
  replaced instances. Corrected.
- **Session timeout** — 15 minutes (a planned, disputed policy as of
  May 2025) versus 10 minutes (IT Security's stated fixed policy, Feb
  2026). Put both on one timeline with explicit dates, without
  asserting how the dispute was resolved — the corpus doesn't say.

## Logged, not changed

These are genuine open questions the corpus doesn't resolve on its own,
not contradictions with a clear right answer:

- **Session-lock participant count** — raw notes say 5 clinicians; the
  participant list says 4 clinicians plus 1 IT person. Both are
  append-only raw files with no arbitration signal in the corpus.
  Needs a decision, not a mechanical fix. *Resolved — see Follow-up
  resolutions §1.*
- **Onboarding raw numbering** — left the raw notes as-is (append-only,
  self-contradictory across sessions) and explained the mismatch in the
  finding instead of editing raw content.
- **Confidence-underline tiers** — the mockup and the raw notes
  describe the underline behavior differently. Needs a decision on
  which is the intended design. *Resolved — see §2.*
- **Onboarding wireflow** — shows the invite step as impossible to
  skip; the research only established that the calendar step is
  required. Needs a decision on intended product behavior. *Resolved —
  see §3.*
- **Flu-season rollout timing** — the rollout went against the training
  team's documented advice to avoid Nov–Feb. This is a decision on
  record, not a data conflict — left as-is.
- **"Per the December retro" framing** — the scope-boundaries finding's
  wording doesn't precisely match what the retro actually praised.
  Worth a closer look, not clearly wrong. *Resolved — see §4.*
- **Old file names retained** — `workspace-setup-step3.md` and
  `ambient-scribe-ga-adoption-week1.md`-style names kept despite content
  changes, since renaming breaks existing links. This has a real cost:
  in round 3, a question leading with "step 3 wireframe" got a wrong
  answer even though both cited excerpts correctly said step 4 — the
  filename itself biased retrieval. Worth a dedicated rename pass as
  its own change. *Resolved — see §5.*
- **Retrieval misses** — some wrong answers weren't contradictions at
  all, just the wrong passage being retrieved (e.g. the adoption window
  and timeout questions). A CRUD UI-side retrieval issue, not a corpus
  issue.

## Not a corpus problem — CRUD UI-side findings

Surfaced during this audit but not corpus issues:

- The model fabricated a "52% report burnout" figure; the source
  actually says 52% name documentation as the top *cause* of burnout.
- The model invented a "69%/31%" readiness split; the corpus says
  34%/71% awareness.
- One question missed existing, relevant ambient scribe data entirely.
- List numbering is stripped during chunking, causing miscounts on any
  numbered list, not just the onboarding wizard.
- Retrieval sometimes surfaces the wrong passage outright, independent
  of any data contradiction.
- A literal filename string biased an answer even when cited excerpts
  were correct.

These point at the RAG endpoint's chunking, retrieval, and prompting on
the CRUD UI side — tracked separately there, not fixed in this corpus.

## Follow-up resolutions (v0.5.27)

Resolves five items from "Logged, not changed" above. The corpus is
fictional demo data, so there is no real researcher or designer to defer
to, and these decisions were made directly. Onboarding raw numbering and
flu-season rollout timing were reviewed and closed without action. They
stay as logged above.

The default rule is the one this audit already uses: **raw notes win over
downstream records.** When a design deliverable or a synthesized finding
says more than the raw evidence supports, the downstream record changes to
match the raw evidence.

### 1. Session-lock participant count

**Conflicting text:**

- `research/raw/2026-02-17-session-lock-during-dictation/session-notes.md`:
  > Contextual inquiry / shadowing, 5 clinicians observed through at least
  > one natural idle-timeout event during a real or simulated dictation
  > session, plus 1 IT Security stakeholder interviewed on the technical
  > constraints
- `participants.md` in the same folder:
  > **Count:** 5 … 4 clinicians recruited from regular ambient-scribe
  > users on units with known short idle-timeout windows; 1 IT Security
  > stakeholder recruited directly for technical context rather than
  > shadowed.

**Decision:** 5 participants in total: 4 clinicians who were shadowed and
1 IT Security stakeholder who was interviewed. The participant list is the
more precise source because it gives a count, a role breakdown, and a
recruitment reason for each group. The session notes most likely counted
the IT stakeholder as a clinician, since "5 clinicians … plus 1 IT" adds up
to 6, which matches neither file.

**How it was applied:** `raw/` is append-only (AGENTS.md), so
`session-notes.md` was **not** edited. Instead,
`correction-2026-09-27.md` was added to the same folder. It quotes both
files and states the correct count. This was a deliberate trade-off.
`build_search_ui.py` and `export_records.py` only read
`session-notes.md`/`participants.md`, so the correction file does not
appear in `search.html` or `/api/ask`, and a raw-note passage can still
surface "5 clinicians." The finding and user flow, which retrieval is meant
to search first, carry the corrected numbers.

**Knock-on effect:** all three records said "4 of 5 clinicians assumed the
draft was lost." Only the 4 clinicians were shadowed through a lock, so the
only consistent reading is **all 4 clinicians**. This makes the headline
stat stronger, but it follows from the count and adds no new claim.
Updated in `research/findings/ambient-scribe-post-ga-refinements.md` (which
also links the correction in its evidence trail) and
`user-flows/ambient-scribe-session-lock-recovery.md`.

### 2. Confidence-underline tiers

**Conflicting text:**

- `mockups/confidence-highlighted-draft-review.md` (hi-fi, dated
  2026-01-05, "built for the Jan 2026 GA-candidate round"):
  > Three-tier treatment: high-confidence lines render as default text
  > with no marking (deliberately inviting skimming); medium-confidence
  > lines get a subtle dotted underline; low-confidence lines get a solid
  > underline plus an on-hover explanation. … the tiers are
  > distinguishable by underline style, not color alone.
- `research/raw/2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate/session-notes.md`:
  > The new per-line confidence highlighting (low-confidence transcription
  > shown in a subtle underline) was well received and, notably, 4 of 6
  > participants said it let them skim high-confidence lines …

**Where they differ:** the mockup has two marked tiers, dotted for medium
and **solid** for low. The raw note describes one marked state, a
**subtle** underline on low-confidence text, and nothing for medium. On
the low tier's style the two descriptions directly contradict each other.

**Decision:** raw wins. The mockup now describes a two-state treatment
(unmarked, or a subtle underline with an on-hover explanation), with no
medium tier.

**Reasoning:** the question was which record describes the tested design,
not which design is better. Four things point to the raw note:
- The mockup says it was built for this test round.
- `prototypes/ambient-scribe-ga-candidate-clickthrough.md` says it was
  both the Jan 13 test build and "the reference build for the
  confidence-highlighted hi-fi mockup."
- Every later record describes one underline: the 2026-02-10
  medication-flag raw note ("the general low-confidence underline"), the
  medication-flag wireframe ("the existing subtle low-confidence
  underline"), and the pre-GA heuristic evaluation ("The generic
  low-confidence underline is the only signal").
- The skimming result is the program's main trust-calibration evidence,
  and it was measured on the binary treatment.

A three-tier treatment might be a reasonable future exploration, but the
mockup presented it as the GA-candidate design, and nothing in the raw
evidence supports that. The hover explanation and the "not color alone"
note were kept because no raw note contradicts them. The mockup's
Overview, which calls this the program's clearest trust-calibration
result, was left unchanged because it is correct for the tested build.

### 3. Onboarding wireflow — invite step skip branch

**Conflicting text:**

- `wireflows/example-wireflow.md` (before):
  > Combines the step 4 ("Invite your team") flow logic with its
  > wireframe, including the branch where a user tries to skip and sees
  > the "required" messaging instead.
  > … Skip attempt branch: user clicks "Skip" -> inline message explains
  > why it can't be skipped -> returns to Step 4.
- `research/raw/2026-01-19-onboarding-usability-test/session-notes.md`:
  > Session 2 — Attempted to skip step 2 directly, assuming it was
  > optional. Backed out when the "required" indicator appeared …
  >
  > Session 3 — … "I guess I'm worried this is going to email my whole
  > team before I'm ready."

  (Session 2's "step 2" is the calendar step. It counts from the step
  after Signup, as covered in "Onboarding step 3" above.)
- `research/findings/onboarding.md`: step 3 ("Connect calendar") is
  "actually required for later functionality"; the step 4 hesitation is
  about whether "invites send immediately or only when the wizard is
  submitted."
- `user-flows/onboarding-flow.md` (canonical v2): "Step 3 — Connect
  calendar (now clearly marked required)"; "Step 4 — Invite your team
  (now shows draft/not-sent state)". It does not mark step 4 as required.

**Decision:** raw wins. In the wireflow, "Skip" on step 4 now advances to
step 5 with nothing drafted or sent, and the wireflow links the onboarding
finding.

**Reasoning:** the only "required" state in the raw notes belongs to the
calendar step. The wireflow appears to have moved that interaction onto
the invite step. The research concern about the invite step is timing
(does it send now?), which the draft/not-sent state already addresses, and
not whether the step is required. Making the step skippable is still an
inference: the research doesn't show that step 4 is optional, only that
nothing makes it required. It is the reading the canonical user flow
supports, because that flow singles out step 3 as required and not step 4.
It also avoids a real design risk, since blocking a skip on a step that
users already find anxious ("email my whole team before I'm ready") would
make that anxiety worse. If product later decides invites should be
mandatory, that is a new decision and needs its own evidence.

### 4. "Per the December retro" framing

**Conflicting text:**

- `research/findings/scope-boundaries-and-workflow-fit.md` (before):
  > Three sessions across the year are grouped here because their common
  > output was a "don't build this yet, or don't build it here"
  > conclusion — which the December executive retro specifically praised
  > as valuable in its own right.
  >
  > Recommendation: keep these three exclusions as explicit, documented
  > scope boundaries in the 2026 roadmap (per the December retro) …
- `research/raw/2025-12-16-stakeholder-interview-executive-steering-committee-retro/session-notes.md`:
  > VP Clinical Informatics specifically praised the decision to keep
  > behavioral health and complex prior-auth cases out of scope for 2025,
  > saying the discipline of 'not doing it' was as valuable as anything
  > shipped.
  >
  > "The stuff you told us not to build yet mattered as much as the stuff
  > you told us to build."

  Recommendations in that note: (1) formalize the two 2026 headline
  metrics; (2) add a standing cross-cutting findings section.

**Where they differ:** the finding's three exclusions are ED intake,
SSO/MFA, and behavioral health. The retro names behavioral health and
complex prior-auth. They overlap on one item, and the retro never mentions
ED intake or SSO/MFA. The retro also made no recommendation about
documenting scope boundaries in the roadmap, so "(per the December retro)"
attributes the finding's own recommendation to the executives.

**Decision:** raw wins, but the change is to narrow the wording, not to
retract it. The overview now says the retro praised scope discipline in
general (with the quote), names the two exclusions it actually cited, and
says it did not discuss ED intake or SSO/MFA. The recommendation is now
presented as this finding's own. The retro was added to the evidence
trail, where it had been missing even though the finding relied on it.

**Reasoning:** the audit called this "not clearly wrong," and that was
fair. The retro does endorse the general idea of "don't build it yet," and
the quote supports that. The problem is precision. "Specifically praised"
and "per the December retro" both suggest the executives endorsed these
three boundaries and asked for them in the roadmap, and they did neither.
That kind of overstatement is how a retrieval answer ends up saying
"executives approved excluding ED intake." The general endorsement was
kept and the specific claims were removed.

for the topline). No test or script in this repo references the old IDs,
but any external bookmark or CRUD UI state keyed on them will not resolve.
