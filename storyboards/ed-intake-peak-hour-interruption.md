## Scenario
**Evidence basis:** Mixed. Observed (the scenario and panels 4–5, from `raw/2025-04-22-contextual-inquiry-ed-intake-shadowing/session-notes.md:24,29,32,35,42,47` and `raw/2026-05-19-interview-ed-charge-nurse-shift-oversight/session-notes.md:30`); illustrative (panels 1–2 are invented scenes, and panel 3 is a concept; whether the pre-fill has been piloted is not recorded, per `raw/2026-04-14-interview-ed-intake-nurse-interruptions-and-downtime/session-notes.md:49`).

A registration clerk and an intake nurse work a peak-hour ED shift where interruptions land every
2-4 minutes — paged, walked up to, or called over.

## Panels
1. *(Illustrative.)* Nurse starts intake on a new patient; gets interrupted by a bedside alarm two questions in.
2. *(Illustrative.)* Returns, has lost track of where they were in the form — a tool requiring sustained review
   attention would compound this, not help it.
3. *(Concept; the pre-fill isn't recorded as piloted.)* Registration clerk, a lower-acuity task, snaps a photo of an insurance card — pre-fill happens
   instantly, no sustained attention required.
4. Contrast panel: the clerk's task fits an AI assist well; the nurse's doesn't — not because of
   the AI's quality, but because of the interruption rate itself.
5. Recommendation callout: carve out insurance-card pre-fill as a smaller, separate, lower-risk
   feature; deprioritize AI features aimed at ED intake nurses during peak hours.
