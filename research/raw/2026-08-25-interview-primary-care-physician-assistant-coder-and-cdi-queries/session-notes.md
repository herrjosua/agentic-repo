# Interview — Primary Care Physician Assistant on Coder and CDI Queries

## Objective
Queries on notes have come up only from the HIM side. A medical coder said that when scribe-drafted notes leave out the specificity coding depends on, they either query the physician or code to the unspecified level, and that they are doing both more often than with notes physicians typed from their own templates (2026-03-10-interview-medical-coder-coding-from-ai-drafted-notes/session-notes.md:31). The same coder's impression, with no count, was that queries back to physicians have gone up since GA and that physicians are slower to answer queries about notes "they didn't really write" (same file, :33). A CDI specialist who writes physician queries (2026-05-12-interview-cdi-specialist-documentation-gaps/session-notes.md:28) said that scribe-drafted notes read as complete, so what is missing from them is harder to spot (same file, :31), and that a physician sometimes gets a second, near-duplicate query from the coder when a CDI query is already open, an annoyance they said predates the scribe (same file, :33). Among raw sessions dated on or before 2026-08-25, queries are mentioned only in those two sessions and in the 2026-08-11 HIM supervisor interview (2026-08-11-interview-him-supervisor-coding-team-oversight/session-notes.md:33), and no session asked a physician, NP or PA what it is like to receive one. This session asked a physician assistant in an ambulatory primary care clinic, about seven months after the January 2026 GA, what the coder and CDI queries they receive on scribe-drafted notes ask for and how they answer them.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a physician assistant in an ambulatory primary care clinic, about eight years in that role. They opted in to the ambient scribe after the January 2026 GA and use it for most visits. The scribe drafts the visit note only; they review the draft, correct single lines in place, and sign. Low-confidence transcription is shown with a subtle underline. Only queries on visit notes were discussed. They did not take part in any earlier session, and the researcher did not share what other participants had said. This is an interview about their own practice; nothing was shown or observed.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(what queries ask)* In their account, most of the queries they get on scribe-drafted notes ask for something they decided at the visit but that the signed note doesn't state outright: whether a condition is acute or chronic, or whether one condition is due to another. The draft recorded the discussion around it, and they signed it without noticing the statement was missing. They put the queries at "two or three a week", an informal impression, not a count.
- **[HIGH]** *(answering later)* Queries reach them days after the visit, sometimes more than a week. To answer one they reread the note and the chart. Because the note reads as complete, they can't always tell from it whether a point was settled at the visit or only talked about, and they then depend on memory. When they can't recall, they say so in the answer rather than guess. They said answering a query on a scribe-drafted note takes them longer than one on a note they typed themselves, because they first have to work out what the note means; that is their impression, and nothing was timed.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(what they check now)* Since the first queries came back, they check before signing that the assessment states acuity and any link between conditions, and add a short line of their own when the draft leaves it implied. That is their account of their own practice, not an observed change, and whether it has changed how many queries they get is not known.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(two queries)* Now and then they get two queries about the same visit, one from CDI and one from coding, asking nearly the same thing, and they answer both. They did not know whether the two teams can see each other's queries.
- **[LOW]** *(physician assistant)* They did not describe anything about receiving or answering queries that works differently for them as a physician assistant. Whether queries reach PAs differently from physicians or NPs is not something one interview can show.

## Representative Quotes
> "At the visit I knew it was chronic. The note talks all around it and never says so, and a week later the query asks me which it is."
> — Physician Assistant (primary care), P152

> "If I can't remember, I write that I can't remember. I'm not going to guess at what I meant a week ago."
> — Physician Assistant (primary care), P152

## Recommendations
1. Share with the ambient scribe team that, in this physician assistant's account, the queries they receive on scribe-drafted notes mostly ask for acuity and for the link between conditions, which the draft records as discussion without stating.
2. When the specificity elements coders and CDI query most often are collected, ask signers as well which of those the draft tends to leave implied, so the list covers both sides of a query.
3. Get query counts and turnaround times, split by scribe-drafted and typed notes. The coder and CDI sessions asked for counts (2026-03-10-interview-medical-coder-coding-from-ai-drafted-notes/session-notes.md:48; 2026-05-12-interview-cdi-specialist-documentation-gaps/session-notes.md:49), and none are recorded in raw on or before this session.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How often queries come and how long they take to answer is not measured; "two or three a week" is one physician assistant's impression.
- Whether physicians and NPs receive the same kinds of queries on scribe-drafted notes is not known; this is one interview.
- Whether checking acuity and condition links before signing changes how many queries come back is untested.
