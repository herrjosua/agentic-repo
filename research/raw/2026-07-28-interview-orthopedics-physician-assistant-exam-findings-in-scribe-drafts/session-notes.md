# Interview — Orthopedics Physician Assistant on Exam Findings in Scribe Drafts

## Objective
No physician assistant has been quoted in an earlier session. Physician assistants are listed on the rosters of the 2025-05-06 dashboard test, the 2025-09-09 baseline survey and the 2026-01-13 GA release-candidate test (participants.md:24, :23 and :25 in those folders), but no quote in any raw session is attributed to one. Laterality in scribe-drafted notes has come up only from the reader's side: a medical coder said these notes often leave out the specificity coding depends on, laterality among it (2026-03-10-interview-medical-coder-coding-from-ai-drafted-notes/session-notes.md:31), and a revenue cycle analyst named a missing laterality among the specificity problems behind coding-related claim denials (2026-07-14-interview-revenue-cycle-analyst-claim-denials/session-notes.md:31). No raw session dated on or before this one mentions orthopedics, range of motion or exam measurements, and none asked the person who signs a draft how exam findings spoken during a hands-on exam come through. This session asked a physician assistant in an orthopedics clinic what is hard to get right in a visit-note draft when findings are spoken while their hands are busy, and what they check before they sign.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a physician assistant in an ambulatory orthopedics clinic, about six years in that role. They opted in to the ambient scribe after the January 2026 GA and use it for most visits. The scribe drafts the visit note only; they review the draft, correct single lines in place, and sign. Low-confidence transcription is shown with a subtle underline. Only the visit note was discussed. They did not take part in any earlier session. This is an interview about their own practice; nothing was shown or observed.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(side)* They say findings out loud while examining, with their hands on the patient and their back to the workstation, and often say "this knee" or "the other side" rather than naming it; patients point and say "this one" too. In their account the draft sometimes leaves the side out of an exam line, or gives every line the side of the main complaint when they examined both. These lines are usually not underlined, because the words were heard clearly. Before signing they check the side on every exam line and in the assessment. They put "a side to fix" at "maybe one note in ten", an informal impression, not a count.
- **[HIGH]** *(measurements)* Range of motion and other measurements come out in a run while they are holding the limb ("flexion one-twenty, extension minus five"). They said the draft sometimes attaches a number to the wrong movement or drops the minus, and only some of those lines are underlined. They check every number in the exam against what they found. Since GA they say each movement and its number as a pair, and name the side, so the draft has less to work out; that is their account of their own practice, not an observed change.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(what they check)* Their review has a fixed order: side first, then every number in the exam, then the assessment and plan. History sections they read more quickly. They put the review at "a couple of minutes a note" on a routine visit, an informal estimate.
- **[LOW]** *(physician assistant)* They did not describe anything about reviewing drafts that works differently for them as a physician assistant. Whether PAs review drafts differently from physicians or NPs is not something one interview can show.

## Representative Quotes
> "My hands are on the knee, not the keyboard. I say 'this side', and the draft has to work out which side that was."
> — Physician Assistant (orthopedics clinic), P151

> "Flexion one-twenty, extension minus five. If it drops the minus, the note says something I didn't find."
> — Physician Assistant (orthopedics clinic), P151

## Recommendations
1. Share with the ambient scribe team that, in this physician assistant's account, the side and exam measurements are where exam lines go wrong without being underlined, because the words are heard clearly.
2. When drafts are evaluated, include hands-on exams where the side is said as "this side" and measurements are spoken in a run, and check side and numbers line by line.
3. In any later training for specialty clinics, include naming the side and saying each measurement with its movement; whether that reduces corrections is untested.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How often the side or a measurement needs correcting is not measured; "maybe one note in ten" is one physician assistant's impression.
- Whether other PAs, NPs or physicians in orthopedics or other hands-on specialties see the same is not known; this is one interview.
- Whether the laterality gaps a medical coder and a revenue cycle analyst described in signed notes are the same gaps this participant corrects before signing is not known.
