# Survey — Patient Trust in AI Chatbot for Scheduling

## Objective
Gauge patient comfort with an AI chatbot handling appointment scheduling and basic pre-visit questions, ahead of building a prototype.

## Method
- **Method:** Online survey via patient portal, 9 questions, incentivized ($10 gift card)
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder

## Key Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** *(trust threshold)* 67% of respondents were comfortable with an AI chatbot for scheduling/rescheduling specifically, but comfort dropped to 22% when the question extended to 'answering questions about my symptoms.'
- **[MEDIUM]** *(transparency ask)* Open-ended responses frequently (41% of those who answered) asked for a clear, easy way to reach 'a real person' if the bot couldn't help — treated as a trust prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.
- **[LOW]** *(age variance)* Comfort with the chatbot skewed higher among respondents who self-reported as under 45, consistent with general digital-trust patterns, not a novel finding but worth noting for pilot recruitment.

## Representative Quotes
> "Booking an appointment, sure, a robot's fine. Asking it if my chest pain is serious? Absolutely not."
> — Patient portal user, P44 (survey open-ended)

> "As long as I can type 'human please' and get one, I don't mind trying the bot first."
> — Patient portal user, P58 (survey open-ended)

## Recommendations
1. Scope the chatbot's initial functionality strictly to scheduling/logistics; explicitly exclude symptom-related Q&A from v1 given the sharp trust drop-off.
2. Design a highly visible, low-friction 'talk to a person' escape hatch as a core requirement, not an afterthought.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- Usability test the escape-hatch design specifically once a prototype exists.
- Consider a targeted follow-up with the 45+ demographic to understand specific hesitations.
