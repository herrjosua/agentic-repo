# Interview — Care Coordinator on Chart Review of Scribe-Drafted Notes

## Objective
Two months after the ambient scribe went GA in January 2026, understand how pre-outreach chart review has changed for a care coordinator now that many of the visit notes they read are scribe-drafted, and whether the "what changed since last review" idea from the January 2025 baseline still describes what they need. Care coordinators don't use the scribe themselves; they read the signed notes clinicians produce with it.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a care coordinator on an ambulatory primary care team who makes outreach calls to a panel of patients and reviews each chart before calling. They did not take part in the January 2025 baseline or the August 2025 alert-triage test. The chart-review summary panel ("what changed since last review") is still a concept and hasn't been built; they were described the idea in this session, not shown screens.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(scribe-drafted notes)* Since GA, the visit notes they read before an outreach call are longer and more conversational than the notes clinicians used to type. What they need for the call (the plan, follow-up items, medication changes) is in there but takes longer to find, so they now jump straight to the plan and the medication list and only read the rest if something there doesn't make sense. They put the extra time at "a couple of extra minutes" on complex patients. This is their informal impression, not something timed in this session.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(what changed)* Without being prompted with the January 2025 quote, they asked for a view of "what changed since my last outreach call". They anchored it on their own last contact with the patient, not on the patient's last visit, because a patient may have had several visits, results or medication changes since the coordinator last spoke with them. This refines the "what changed since last review" concept: the reference point that matters to them is the coordinator's last contact.

## Representative Quotes
> "The notes are longer now. It's all in there, it just reads like a conversation, so I skip to the plan and the med list and work backwards if I have to."
> — Care Coordinator, P130

> "I don't need to know what happened at the last visit. I need to know what happened since the last time I talked to them."
> — Care Coordinator, P130

## Recommendations
1. Anchor the chart-review summary concept on the coordinator's last outreach contact, not only on the last visit, and test both anchors when the concept is next put in front of coordinators.
2. Share with the ambient scribe team that downstream readers who aren't clinicians (coordinators, as well as coders) rely on the plan and medication sections being easy to find in scribe-drafted notes.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How much longer chart review takes on scribe-drafted notes is not measured. The January 2025 baseline's 8-14 minutes per patient hasn't been re-timed since GA; a timed observation would be needed to check the "couple of extra minutes" impression.
- Does "last outreach contact" exist as a recorded event anywhere a summary could read it, or only in the coordinator's own notes and the shared spreadsheet?
