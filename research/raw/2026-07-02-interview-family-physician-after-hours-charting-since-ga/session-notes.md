# Interview — Family Physician on After-Hours Charting Since GA

## Objective
The September 2025 baseline survey asked about after-hours charting and reported it for physician respondents (2025-09-09-survey-clinician-burnout-documentation-burden-baseline/session-notes.md:29). It recommended repeating the same instrument six months after the ambient scribe GA (same file, :40, :44). No repeat is recorded in research/raw on or before this session's date: the only survey folder after September 2025 is the December 2025 all-staff AI readiness pulse (2025-12-02-survey-allstaff-ai-readiness-pulse). No earlier 1:1 session asked a physician about charting after clinic: among raw sessions dated on or before 2026-07-02, after-hours and "pajama time" charting appear only in the September survey (session-notes.md:29, :40). This session asked a family physician, about six months after the January 2026 GA, when and how they finish their notes now. It is one physician's informal account and does not re-measure anything the survey measured.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a family medicine physician in an ambulatory primary care clinic, about ten years in practice. They opted in to the ambient scribe shortly after the January 2026 GA and use it for most visits. The scribe drafts the visit note only; the clinician reviews the draft, corrects single lines in place, and signs, and enters orders separately. They did not take part in any earlier session; whether they responded to the September 2025 survey is not known, because it was anonymous. This is an interview about their own practice; nothing was shown or observed, and nothing was timed.

## Key Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** *(evening notes)* They still finish some notes after clinic on most days. In their account the evening work is now mostly reading and signing drafts they didn't get to between visits, plus the notes for visits where they chose not to use the scribe; before the scribe, they recalled, it was mostly writing notes from memory. They put it at "four or five notes on a full day", an informal estimate. They have not timed it, then or now, and this is not a measure of time saved.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(which notes wait)* The drafts that wait until the evening are the ones that need more than a quick read: long visits with several problems, and drafts where they expect to move or rewrite lines. Short visits they try to sign straight after the patient leaves. When the clinic runs late, that falls apart and more drafts wait.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(reviewing later)* Reviewing a draft hours after the visit is harder for them than reviewing it straight away, because checking it depends on remembering what was said. In the evening they read more slowly and leave more of it as written when they can't recall the detail. That is their own description of their practice; how often it happens was not discussed.
- **[LOW]** *(other evening work)* They said much of their evening now goes to patient messages and test results rather than notes, and the scribe doesn't touch those. That is their impression; how their evening time divides was not measured.

## Representative Quotes
> "The evening used to be writing. Now it's mostly reading what it wrote and signing, and then the messages."
> — Physician (Family Medicine), P150

> "Right after the visit I remember what we said. By the evening I'm checking it against a memory that's gone fuzzy."
> — Physician (Family Medicine), P150

## Recommendations
1. Treat this interview as context for the repeat of the September 2025 survey, not as a measure. Whether after-hours charting has changed since GA needs that repeat, which the baseline recommended and which is not recorded in the corpus.
2. Share with the ambient scribe team that, in this physician's account, a draft reviewed hours after the visit gets a weaker check, because the check depends on memory of the visit.
3. In later after-hours questions, ask about patient messages and test results as well as notes, since this physician placed much of their evening work there.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- Whether after-hours charting has changed since GA is not measured; this is one physician's informal account, and no repeat of the September 2025 survey is recorded in the corpus.
- How their evening time divides between notes, messages and results is not measured.
- Whether other physicians, or NPs and PAs, describe the same pattern is not known; this is one interview.
