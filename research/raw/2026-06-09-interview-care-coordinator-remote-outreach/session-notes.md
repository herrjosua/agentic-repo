# Interview — Remote Care Coordinator on Telephonic Outreach

## Objective
Understand how pre-outreach chart review works for a care coordinator who works fully remotely and does all their outreach by phone, and whether the January 2025 baseline's two workarounds (cross-checking 3-4 systems before every call, and an undocumented colour-coding system in the shared spreadsheet) look different from home.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a care coordinator who works fully remotely and makes telephonic outreach calls to a panel of patients. They read the signed visit notes clinicians produce, including scribe-drafted ones, but don't use the scribe themselves. Alerts still arrive in the chronological queue; the AI-ranked queue hasn't been built. The chart-review summary panel is still a concept and wasn't shown in this session.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(cross-checking systems)* Before each call they still cross-check the same 3 to 4 systems the January 2025 baseline described: the EHR, the care management platform, the shared spreadsheet and the legacy scheduling tool. Working from home, the legacy scheduling tool is the slowest of the four to load and to move around in, so it is the step they most often put off until they're already on the phone. This is their own account; nothing was timed in this session.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(undocumented colour key)* They keep a private notes document alongside the shared spreadsheet, because the spreadsheet's colour key isn't written down anywhere. They learned what the colours mean by asking colleagues when they started, and wrote the answers into their own document. This is the baseline's undocumented-workaround pattern again, now with a second, private layer on top of it.

## Representative Quotes
> "It's still the same four places before every call. From home, the scheduling one is the one that makes me wait."
> — Care Coordinator, P133

> "Nobody wrote the colours down. I asked around, and now I've got my own little document that says what they mean."
> — Care Coordinator, P133

## Recommendations
1. When the chart-review summary concept is next tested, include remote coordinators and check how it handles data from the legacy scheduling tool, since that is the slowest source for them.
2. Treat the spreadsheet's colour key as information the design has to carry (or replace), not a private convention each coordinator rebuilds for themselves.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How much slower the legacy scheduling tool is from home than on site is not measured; this is one coordinator's impression.
- How many coordinators keep a private key to the spreadsheet, as this participant does, is not known.
