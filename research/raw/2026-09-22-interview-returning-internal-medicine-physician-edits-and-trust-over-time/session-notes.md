# Interview — Returning Internal Medicine Physician on Edits and Trust Over Time

## Objective
The participant is P09, returning from 2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate. In the v0.1 test they said they had to read every word anyway (2025-02-25-usability-test-ambient-scribe-v01/session-notes.md:36-37). In the v0.2 test they said they still read every line, and that getting it right once didn't mean they trusted it yet (2025-09-23-usability-test-ambient-scribe-v02/session-notes.md:36-37). At the GA release-candidate round they said it was the first time they had skimmed a section instead of reading every word, because it showed them where to look (2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate/session-notes.md:36-37); that round recorded 4 of 6 participants saying the per-line underline let them skim high-confidence lines (same file, :30). This interview is in September 2026. The questions were what they edit in drafts now, what they read in full and what they skim, and what in their account has changed or stayed the same since January.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is an internal medicine physician in an ambulatory clinic. They have used the ambient scribe since the January 2026 GA. The scribe drafts the visit note only; they review the draft, correct single lines in place, and sign. Low-confidence transcription is shown with a subtle underline. Their earlier sessions were moderated usability tests; this is an interview about their own practice, nothing was shown or observed, and what they describe can't be compared directly with what those tests recorded. The researcher did not share what other participants had said.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(order of events)* In their account, most of their edits now are not misheard words. The draft sometimes gets the order of events in the history wrong: which symptom came first, or how long ago something started. Those lines are usually not underlined, because each word was heard clearly. They put it at "a few notes most weeks", an informal impression, not a count.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(what they skim)* They still skim history sections where nothing is underlined on routine follow-up visits, and read the assessment and plan in full on every draft. In January they said the underline let them skim a section for the first time (2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate/session-notes.md:36); what they read in full that day is not recorded, so the two can't be compared line by line.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(new problems)* After catching, during review in the spring, a draft whose history put two symptoms in the wrong order with nothing underlined, they began reading the whole history whenever a visit is about a new problem. That is their account of a change in their own practice since January, not an observed change.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(trust by section)* Asked whether they trust the scribe more than in January, they said it depends on the section: they rely on it to have heard the words, and they check that the history and the plan say what happened and what they decided. They did not describe their trust as having grown overall, and this interview does not measure trust.
- **[LOW]** *(edit count)* They put their edits at "one or two on most notes", an informal estimate.

## Representative Quotes
> "It hears the words. What I check is whether the story's in the right order."
> — Physician, Internal Medicine, P09 (returning from 2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate)

> "Do I trust it more than in January? For some sections. Not the plan, and not the history of a new problem."
> — Physician, Internal Medicine, P09 (returning from 2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate)

## Recommendations
1. Share with the ambient scribe team that, in this physician's account, the order of events in the history is where drafts go wrong without being underlined, because each word is heard clearly.
2. When drafts are evaluated, check the sequence of events in the history against the visit, not only word accuracy.
3. If the team wants to know whether review habits change over time, plan a measure for it; one returning participant's account can't show a trend.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How often the order of events is wrong is not measured; "a few notes most weeks" is one physician's impression.
- Whether other clinicians read the history in full for new problems is not known; this is one interview.
- Whether skimming has changed since January is not measured; the January round was a moderated test and this is an interview.
