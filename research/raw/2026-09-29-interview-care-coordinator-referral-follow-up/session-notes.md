# Interview — Specialty Clinic Care Coordinator on Referral Follow-up

## Objective
Understand how a care coordinator on a specialty clinic team follows up referrals, where the time goes, and what a 'what changed' view would need to cover for them, following up the January 2025 baseline's recommendation to scope early AI work around 'what changed since last review'.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a care coordinator on a specialty clinic team whose main work is following up referrals. They read the signed visit notes clinicians produce, including scribe-drafted ones, but don't use the scribe themselves. Alerts still arrive in the chronological queue; the AI-ranked queue hasn't been built. The chart-review summary panel is still a concept and wasn't shown in this session. They did not take part in any earlier session.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(confirming referrals)* Their biggest time sink is confirming in the legacy scheduling tool whether a referred appointment actually happened. Overdue-referral alerts are often already resolved by the time they reach them, because the appointment took place but the alert wasn't cleared, so they spend time checking alerts that need no action. This is their account; nothing was timed and no alert data was reviewed in this session.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(what changed)* A 'what changed' view would need to cover appointments and results, not just medications. For referral follow-up, a new appointment or a returned result is usually the change that matters. This extends the January 2025 baseline's 'what changed since last review' idea, which grew out of medication changes.

## Representative Quotes
> "Most of my day is going into the scheduling system to see if they actually went. Half the overdue alerts, they already did."
> — Care Coordinator, P137

> "Meds are only part of it. For me, what changed means did they get seen, and did the results come back."
> — Care Coordinator, P137

## Recommendations
1. When the chart-review summary concept is next tested, check whether it can show appointment status from the legacy scheduling tool, so referral follow-up doesn't need a separate lookup.
2. Scope 'what changed' to include appointments and results as well as medications, and test that scope with coordinators who do referral follow-up.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How many overdue-referral alerts are already resolved when a coordinator reaches them is not measured; this is one coordinator's account.
- How often after-visit summaries miss changes is still not measured beyond the January 2025 baseline's account.
- Real-world triage speed, with or without a ranked queue, has not been measured.
