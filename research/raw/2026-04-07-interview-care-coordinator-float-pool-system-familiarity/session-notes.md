# Interview — Float-Pool Care Coordinator on System Familiarity

## Objective
Follow up the January 2025 baseline's open question on float-pool coordinators, who "may have different pain points due to less system familiarity". The participant is P05, returning from 2025-01-29-contextual-inquiry-chart-review-baseline, where they were shadowed in January 2025; this interview is in April 2026. The question was what covering for different teams does to chart review and alert triage, and whether their own 2025 request ("what changed since I last touched this chart") still fits how they work.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant still works in the float pool, covering ambulatory care coordination teams when their own coordinators are out. Alerts still arrive in the chronological queue; the AI-ranked queue tested in August 2025 hasn't been built, and they did not take part in that test. The chart-review summary panel is still a concept.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(float pool)* Each team they cover keeps its own version of the shared spreadsheet, with its own columns and its own colour-coding, and none of it is written down. They spend the start of a covering shift working out that team's conventions, from the sheet itself or by asking whoever is on the team that day, before they can trust what the sheet is telling them about a patient. This is the undocumented-workaround pattern from the January 2025 baseline, seen from the side of the person who has to read it.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(what changed)* "Since I last touched this chart" doesn't fit floating, because on a covering shift they have often never touched the chart. What they want is "what changed since the team's last contact" with the patient, whoever made it. This refines their own January 2025 request rather than replacing it: the reference point is the last contact by anyone on the care team.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(alert triage)* When floating, they work the alert queue strictly in order. They said they don't know the patients well enough to move one alert above another on history, so they leave reprioritising to the team's own coordinators when they're back. This is their account of their own practice; nothing was observed or measured in this session.

## Representative Quotes
> "Every team has their own sheet and their own colours. The first half hour of a covering day is me figuring out what yellow means on this team."
> — Care Coordinator (float pool), P05 (returning from 2025-01-29-contextual-inquiry-chart-review-baseline)

> "When I'm covering, I haven't touched the chart. I need to know what's changed since anybody on the team last talked to this person."
> — Care Coordinator (float pool), P05 (returning from 2025-01-29-contextual-inquiry-chart-review-baseline)

## Recommendations
1. In the chart-review summary concept, support "since the team's last contact" as well as "since my last contact", so it works for covering coordinators.
2. Treat each team's spreadsheet conventions as something the design has to account for (or replace), not a single shared convention.
3. When AI ranking is next tested, include float-pool coordinators specifically, since they may rely on the ranked order differently from coordinators who know the patients.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How many teams' spreadsheet conventions differ, and how much, is not known; this is one coordinator's account.
- Would a covering coordinator follow an AI-ranked order more closely than a team's own coordinator does? Not tested; the August 2025 test did not separate float-pool participants.
