# Interview — Utilization Review Nurse on Checking Citations

## Objective
`prior-authorization.md` says nurses want inline citations to the chart text behind each part of the draft, and that in v2 the citations were used rather than ignored, with 3 of 4 participants clicking into at least one citation before accepting (`findings/prior-authorization.md`:30-34). Both claims come from the April 2025 v1 and November 2025 v2 tests, whose participants asked for the citations or tested them. This session asked a utilization review nurse who joined the department after both tests, and has only ever used the panel in the pilot, whether they want the citations and how they check them, and how they handle the cases the panel doesn't take, including claim denials and calls with the health plan.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** A limited pilot of the prior-auth drafting panel has run in the utilization review department since early 2026, on straightforward cases only; complex cases stay manual. The pilot is not generally available and has not reached other departments. The panel shows inline citations to the chart entries behind each part of the draft. The participant joined the department after the November 2025 v2 test and did not take part in any earlier session, so nothing here speaks to v1 or v2. They use the panel on their own straightforward cases. They are not P19, the utilization review nurse from v1 and June 2026, and not P17, the utilization review supervisor from v1 and v2. No pilot outcomes data was reviewed in this session, and the written straightforward-case definition was not reviewed either.

## Key Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** *(citations as the way to read a draft)* They want the citations, and don't think of them as an added feature: they have never used a draft without them, and opening them is how they read a draft at all. On each case they open the citation behind every criterion the request has to meet and read the chart entry it points to. What they check is whether that entry says what the draft says it says, not only that it exists; their example was a cited note that supports a symptom the draft describes as ongoing, when the note is months old. On their informal impression, most drafts need no change after checking and some need a sentence reworded; they kept no count. This confirms `prior-authorization.md`:30-34 for a nurse who wasn't in v1 or v2: the citations are wanted and used in pilot work, not only in testing. It also complicates the v2 measure: "clicked into at least one citation" records that a citation was opened, not what the nurse checked once it was.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(manual cases, claim denials and calls)* Cases outside the pilot's straightforward scope they do by hand from the start. For a request that follows a claim denial, they begin from the denial letter's stated reason and then gather the chart evidence that answers it; in their view a draft built from the chart would start in the wrong place, because the reason is in the letter, not the chart. Before a call with the health plan about a case, they prepare by hand too, keeping their own list of chart references to have open during the call. They described the calls as the hardest part of the week to plan for, but gave no time figure. For a straightforward case that the health plan later asks about, they said they find the evidence again from scratch; they didn't know whether a submitted request keeps the panel's citations.

## Representative Quotes
> "I've never had a draft without them. The citations aren't extra, they're how I read the thing."
> — Utilization Review Nurse, P144

> "A denial comes with a reason, and the reason's in the letter, not the chart. So I start from the letter and go find what answers it."
> — Utilization Review Nurse, P144

## Recommendations
1. When `prior-authorization.md` is next revised, note that the citation requirement holds for a nurse who joined during the pilot, and that what they check is whether the cited text supports the draft's wording.
2. If citation use is measured in the pilot, record what nurses check in a citation, not only whether they open one.
3. Ask the pilot team whether a submitted request keeps its citations, so a nurse can reuse them if the health plan asks about the case later.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How the pilot is performing is not known: no outcomes data (time, accuracy, claim denials) is recorded in the corpus, none was reviewed here, and the participant had not seen any figures. The only time figures on record are v1's estimate and v2's measurement on 2 straightforward test cases.
- Whether a submitted request keeps its citations is not known; the participant didn't know.
- How many drafts need changing after the citations are checked is not counted; "most" needing no change is one nurse's impression.
- Whether other pilot users check citations the same way is not known; this is one interview.
- Whether drafting could help with requests that follow a claim denial is untested; the participant does them by hand, and nothing here supports bringing them into the pilot.
