# Interview — Nurse Manager on Unit Champions

## Objective
`rollout-and-change-management.md` recommends a unit-champion model (1-2 respected staff per unit, trained early and deeply) plus mandatory hands-on practice time (`findings/rollout-and-change-management.md`:33-36). That recommendation comes from the training department (2025-10-21-interview-training-department-rollout-change-management). This session asked a nurse manager, who would have to release those nurses from the unit, what it takes to give a champion real practice time, and what they would need before they could release a nurse for training.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is the nurse manager of an inpatient medical-surgical unit: staffing, the schedule, and the unit's training and competencies. No AI feature is used by the nurses on their unit; the ambient scribe, GA since January 2026, drafts notes for physicians, NPs and PAs only, and the champion training reported in the January 2026 release-candidate test was for scribe users (2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate/session-notes.md:32). What they said draws on releasing nurses for other training, such as EHR updates and annual competencies, not on an AI rollout. They did not take part in any earlier session. They are not P99, the Director of Clinical Training & Education interviewed in October 2025, and not P141, the medical-surgical night nurse interviewed in July 2026; whether P141 works on their unit was not asked.

## Key Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** *(release time)* Staffing makes protected practice time hard to keep. Releasing a nurse for a training session means backfilling their assignment with a float nurse or overtime, and on a day when census is high or someone calls out, training is the first thing they cancel, because patient assignments can't be. They agreed with the champion model in principle; the gap is that whether champions are "given actual time to practice" (2025-10-21-interview-training-department-rollout-change-management/session-notes.md:34) depends on the unit's staffing on the day. This complicates the recommendation in `rollout-and-change-management.md`: the model assumes practice time is available, and on this unit it is the thing most likely to be lost. It is consistent with the finding's advice to avoid flu season, which they also named as their hardest months to release anyone.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(what release needs)* What they said they would need to release a nurse: training dates known before they build the schedule, which they do about six weeks out (their description of their own practice); backfill paid from a training budget rather than the unit's; sessions offered on nights and weekends too, because night nurses otherwise miss them; and champions given time on the schedule for supporting peers, not expected to do it on top of a full assignment.
- **[LOW]** *(choosing champions)* They would want to pick the champions with the training team rather than have them assigned, and said the nurses who volunteer first are not always the ones others go to for help. This came up once and wasn't explored.

## Representative Quotes
> "Everyone agrees on time to practice. Then census goes up at six in the morning and the first thing I cancel is the class."
> — Nurse Manager (medical-surgical), P142

> "Put it on the schedule when I build it and pay for the backfill. Then I can send somebody."
> — Nurse Manager (medical-surgical), P142

## Recommendations
1. When `rollout-and-change-management.md` is next revised, add release time as a condition of the unit-champion model: practice time has to be scheduled and backfilled in advance, not assumed.
2. For any future rollout that reaches nurses, agree training dates with unit managers before their schedule is built, and include night and weekend sessions.
3. Budget champion time for supporting peers as scheduled time, not extra work on a full assignment.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- Whether the training department can fund backfill for practice time is not known; it was not discussed with the training department.
- How often training is cancelled for staffing on this or other units is not measured; this is one manager's account.
- Whether the champion training for the ambient scribe GA kept its practice time is not recorded; this participant had no nurses in it.
- Whether other nurse managers would release nurses on the same terms is not known; this is one interview.
