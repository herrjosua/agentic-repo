# Interview — HIM Supervisor on Coding Team Oversight

## Objective
Bring the view of someone who manages coders into the HIM research: how a supervisor sets productivity expectations, trains and monitors coders, and what they would expect to change if the AI coding-suggestion concept (July 2025 prototype, not built or piloted) were ever built. This follows the June 2026 coder interview, which recommended raising quotas with HIM leadership. The participant supervises a coding team. They have the same role label as P67 (July 2025 contextual inquiry) and P121 (March 2026 interview), but they are a different person from both and took part in neither session.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The AI coding-suggestion tool is still a concept from July 2025 and has not been built or piloted; 42 CFR Part 2 handling is still manual. The participant supervises outpatient and inpatient coders, sets their daily productivity targets with HIM leadership, onboards new coders and reviews their early work. Their team has coded from scribe-drafted notes since GA. They had not seen the July 2025 screens before this session.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(productivity)* Their main worry about a coding suggester isn't accuracy. It's that leadership would raise coding targets as soon as it went live, on the assumption that AI saves time, before anyone has measured whether it does. They said targets went up once before when a new coding tool came in, and weren't revisited when the expected gain didn't show up on complex charts. They had heard the July 2025 "2-3 minutes per simple chart" estimate second-hand and were concerned it would be applied to every chart. If targets rose first, they expect coders to protect their numbers by accepting suggestions on simple charts, which is the habit the June 2026 coder described.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(training)* They said override behaviour would have to be trained and monitored as a skill, not left to habit. For new coders they would want to see, at least for the first months, how often each person accepts a suggestion unchanged versus edits or overrides it, and to review a sample of the unchanged ones. They saw this as a supervisor's tool, not only an auditor's, and said they can't coach what they can't see. Today they can only see finished codes, not how a coder got there.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(42 CFR Part 2)* Coders aren't the Part 2 decision-makers, but they do see substance-use content in charts and need to know what not to copy into queries or notes to other teams. Part 2 training for coders still depends on memory and a printed sheet. It is a version of the sheet the ROI team uses, which the March 2026 supervisor described, adapted for coding, and nobody owns keeping it current. Nothing has changed since GA, though scribe-drafted notes capture more of that content.

## Representative Quotes
> "The day this goes live, someone upstairs is going to bump the target, because 'the AI does half of it now.' Nobody's going to measure first. They never do."
> — HIM Supervisor, P127

> "I can teach someone to override a bad suggestion. What I can't do is teach it once and assume it sticks. If I can't see who's accepting everything, I'm not supervising, I'm hoping."
> — HIM Supervisor, P127

## Recommendations
1. If the coding-suggestion concept is pursued, agree with HIM leadership in advance that productivity targets stay unchanged during any pilot. Revisit them only after measured time per chart, split by simple and complex charts, and alongside denial and rework rates.
2. Expose per-coder acceptance, edit and override rates to supervisors as well as coding auditors, to support training and coaching rather than only retrospective audit.
3. Add coding to the case for a structured 42 CFR Part 2 flag. Coders rely on the same memory and printed-sheet approach as ROI.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- Which earlier tool rollout led to a target increase, and is there any before-and-after data on coder throughput from it?
- Who owns the coding version of the Part 2 sheet, and when was it last updated?
