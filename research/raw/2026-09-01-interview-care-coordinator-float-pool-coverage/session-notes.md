# Interview — Float Pool Care Coordinator on Covering Several Teams

## Objective
Understand how a float pool care coordinator handles covering several teams in one shift, and how they would react to an AI-ranked alert queue when they don't know the patients' history, following up P05's April 2026 interview on float pool system familiarity.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a float pool care coordinator who covers several teams. They are a different person from P05, the float pool coordinator in the January 2025 baseline and the April 2026 interview. They read the signed visit notes clinicians produce, including scribe-drafted ones, but don't use the scribe themselves. Alerts still arrive in each team's chronological queue; the AI-ranked queue hasn't been built. They reacted to a description of the ranked queue, not to screens. They did not take part in any earlier session.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(no combined view)* On a typical shift they cover several teams and switch between each team's own alert queue, with no view that brings the queues together. They keep track of where they are in each queue themselves. This is their account of their own practice; nothing was observed or measured in this session.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(float vs continuity)* They would accept a ranked queue as a starting point, because as a float coordinator they don't have the patient history that team coordinators use to reorder alerts. P05 said something related in April 2026: when floating, they work the queue strictly in order and leave reprioritising to the team's own coordinators. These are two voices from float coordinators, set against team coordinators (P81 in 2025, P134 in August 2026) who lean on knowing their patients. At n=2 this is a contrast worth testing, not a pattern.

## Representative Quotes
> "I'm in three or four queues a shift, and I'm flipping between them. There's no one place that shows me all of it."
> — Care Coordinator (float pool), P136

> "I don't know these patients' history, so honestly a ranking would be a better start than what I've got. I'd still check it."
> — Care Coordinator (float pool), P136

## Recommendations
1. Explore a combined queue view for coordinators who cover more than one team, and test it with float pool coordinators.
2. When the ranked queue is next tested, include both float and team coordinators so the float-versus-continuity contrast can be checked with more than two float voices.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How many teams a float coordinator typically covers in a shift is not measured; this is one coordinator's account.
- How accurate the 2025 ranking is, and what it was based on, are still not documented, so it isn't known whether accepting it as a starting point is safe.
- How often coordinators would follow a ranked order without their own judgement has not been measured, for float coordinators or anyone else.
