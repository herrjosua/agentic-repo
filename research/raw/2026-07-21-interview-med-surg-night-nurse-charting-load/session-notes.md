# Interview — Med-Surg Night Nurse on Charting Load

## Objective
The documentation-burden finding quantifies after-hours charting for physicians only: the September 2025 baseline found 64% of physician respondents regularly finishing documentation after clinic hours, at a self-reported 1.2 hours a day (2025-09-09-survey-clinician-burnout-documentation-burden-baseline/session-notes.md:30). Nothing in the corpus describes the same thing for nurses beyond the February 2025 nursing survey's attitudes and one respondent's wish to get "out of the room 10 minutes faster to chart" (2025-02-11-survey-nursing-attitudes-ai-documentation/session-notes.md:37). This session asked an inpatient nurse whether their charting runs past the end of their shift, why, and what they expect of AI drafting nursing notes, which doesn't exist.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a registered nurse on an inpatient medical-surgical unit, working twelve-hour night shifts, about two years in; they finished unit orientation as a new graduate. Their charting is manual. The ambient scribe, GA since January 2026, drafts notes for physicians, NPs and PAs only, and no nurse uses it for their own documentation, so everything they said about AI drafting nursing notes is an expectation, not experience. They did not take part in any earlier session, including the February 2025 nursing survey.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(after-shift charting)* Their charting regularly continues after the shift ends. They hand off at the end of the night, then stay at a workstation to finish notes they could not complete during the shift. They put it at "most shifts, thirty to forty-five minutes"; that is an informal self-estimate, not timed or logged. The notes left over are mostly the narrative parts and the end-of-shift reassessments, because the start of the night is admissions and the early morning is medication passes and labs, and charting is what gets deferred whenever a patient needs something.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(burden beyond physicians)* This extends the documentation-burden finding to registered nurses qualitatively: one nurse describes after-shift charting as routine on their unit. It does not supply a nurse figure. The September 2025 figures (52%, 64%, 1.2 hours a day) are physician-only and should not be read as describing nurses; this interview's thirty to forty-five minutes is one nurse's estimate and isn't comparable to them. They linked the after-shift time to how tired they are at the end of a run of nights, but described it as fatigue, not burnout, and no burnout measure was used.
- **[LOW]** *(tenure and skepticism)* Asked about years of experience, they offered their own view as a newer nurse: they haven't settled into a way of charting yet, which might make them more open to a draft, but it also means they would check every word, because they don't yet trust themselves to spot what a draft got wrong. This is one newer nurse's view. It does not answer the February 2025 survey's open question on whether skepticism correlates with tenure, which needs the survey cross-tab.

## Representative Quotes
> "Report's done and I'm still at the workstation finishing my notes. Day shift is already running the floor around me."
> — Registered Nurse (medical-surgical), P141

> "I'm new enough that I don't have a way of charting yet. Maybe that makes me more open to it. It also means I'd read every word, because I don't trust myself to know what it got wrong."
> — Registered Nurse (medical-surgical), P141

## Recommendations
1. When the documentation-burden finding is next revised, note that after-shift charting is also reported by nurses, qualitatively, and keep the September figures labelled as physician-only.
2. If nurse documentation burden needs a number, measure it directly (the September baseline's registered nurse responses reported separately, if they exist, or time logs), rather than extending the physician figures.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How much time nurses spend charting after their shift is not measured; "thirty to forty-five minutes" is one nurse's estimate, on one unit.
- The September 2025 baseline's roster lists registered nurses among its respondents, but its notes report the burnout and after-hours figures for physicians only; whether nurse results exist is not recorded.
- Whether skepticism varies with tenure is still open; the February 2025 survey's cross-tab hasn't been run, and one interview can't answer it.
- Whether day-shift nurses on the same unit chart after their shift too is not known.
