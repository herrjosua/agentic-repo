# Interview — New Care Coordinator on Learning the Queue

## Objective
Understand how a care coordinator who is new to the role learns the team's working conventions, and what an AI-ranked alert queue would mean for someone who doesn't yet have the experience to judge it, following up the August 2025 alert-triage test and the undocumented colour-coding first described in the January 2025 baseline.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a care coordinator on an ambulatory team who has been in the role for under a year. They read the signed visit notes clinicians produce, including scribe-drafted ones, but don't use the scribe themselves. Their team works the chronological queue; the AI-ranked queue hasn't been built. They reacted to a description of the ranked queue, not to screens. They did not take part in any earlier session.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(learning by shadowing)* They learned the team's conventions, including what the shared spreadsheet's colours mean and which alerts can safely wait, by shadowing experienced coordinators, with nothing written down to check against later. They thought a ranked queue might help newer coordinators most, because it would stand in for the judgement they haven't built yet. But they said newer coordinators would also be the least able to tell when the ranking is wrong. This is their own reflection; nothing was observed or measured in this session.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(chart review time)* In their first months, chart review before an outreach call took them "closer to 20 minutes at first", which is longer than the 8-14 minutes the January 2025 baseline described for experienced coordinators. They said it has come down since. This is an informal recollection, not a timed measurement.

## Representative Quotes
> "Nobody gave me a list. I sat next to people for two weeks and picked up what the colours meant and which alerts could wait."
> — Care Coordinator, P135

> "A ranked list would've been great my first month. But I wouldn't have known if it was wrong, and that's the scary part."
> — Care Coordinator, P135

## Recommendations
1. Treat newer coordinators as a distinct group when the ranked queue is next tested: check whether they can spot a wrong ranking, not just whether they work faster with one.
2. Write down the team conventions that are currently passed on by shadowing (colour meanings, which alerts can wait), so that any design that carries or replaces them has a source to work from.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How long chart review takes for coordinators in their first year is not measured; "closer to 20 minutes at first" is one coordinator's recollection.
- How accurate the 2025 ranking is, and what it was based on, are still not documented, so it isn't known how often a newer coordinator would meet a wrong ranking.
- How often coordinators would follow a ranked order without their own judgement has not been measured, for newer coordinators or anyone else.
