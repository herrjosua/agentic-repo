# Interview — Returning Nurse Practitioner on Champion Training and Review Habits

## Objective
At the GA release-candidate round, this participant said they had been through the champion training the month before and "walked in already comfortable" (2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate/session-notes.md:39). That round found that participants who had been through the champion-model style training pilot reported higher initial comfort than those who hadn't, though it wasn't a controlled comparison (same file, :32). The participant is P118, returning from 2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate; this interview is in May 2026. The questions were what the training did and didn't prepare them for once they used the scribe every day, and how they review drafts today.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a nurse practitioner in an ambulatory clinic who has used the ambient scribe since the January 2026 GA. They review and sign their own visit notes. They went through the champion training pilot before the January 2026 round; how clinicians who started using the scribe after GA were trained is not recorded in the corpus, and they did not know. This is an interview about their own practice; nothing was shown or observed.

## Key Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** *(training into practice)* In their account, the pilot training prepared them for the controls, for correcting a single line in place, and for what the underline means, and the first weeks after GA went smoothly for them. That is consistent with what they said in January (2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate/session-notes.md:39). What it didn't prepare them for were visits unlike the practice visits in the training: several people talking, a visit interrupted partway through, or a patient raising a second problem at the door. They learned to handle those drafts by using the scribe. This is one participant's account and says nothing about how much the training helped compared with no training.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(review habits)* They read the assessment and plan in full on every draft. History sections they skim where nothing is underlined. They put their review at "a minute or two a note" on a routine visit, an informal estimate. The January round recorded 4 of 6 participants saying the underline let them skim (2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate/session-notes.md:30) without saying who, so this can't be compared with what this participant did in January, and it isn't described here as a change.
- **[LOW]** *(peer questions)* Colleagues who started using the scribe after GA ask them how to correct a line or what the underline means. They described this as informal and did not describe any formal role.
- **[LOW]** *(nurse practitioner use)* They did not describe anything about the scribe that works differently for them as a nurse practitioner. Whether NPs review drafts differently from physicians is not something one interview can show.

## Representative Quotes
> "The training got me comfortable with the buttons. What it couldn't show me was a visit where three people are talking and the patient remembers a second problem at the door."
> — Nurse Practitioner, P118 (returning from 2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate)

> "I read the plan every time. The history I'll skim if nothing's underlined."
> — Nurse Practitioner, P118 (returning from 2026-01-13-usability-test-ambient-scribe-ga-release-candidate)

## Recommendations
1. In any later ambient-scribe training, include practice with drafts from visits that don't go to plan: several speakers, interruptions, a second problem raised at the end.
2. Record how clinicians who start using the scribe after GA are trained, so any later comparison of trained and untrained clinicians rests on a known basis.
3. Note that informal peer questions are reaching pilot-trained clinicians; if that support is expected of them, it needs time set aside for it.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How clinicians who started using the scribe after GA were trained is not recorded in the corpus.
- Whether pilot-trained clinicians are more comfortable than others is still not a controlled comparison; the January round said so, and one returning participant can't settle it.
- How long draft review takes is not measured; "a minute or two a note" is one nurse practitioner's estimate.
