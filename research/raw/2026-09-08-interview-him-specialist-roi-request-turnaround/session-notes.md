# Interview — HIM Specialist on ROI Request Turnaround

## Objective
Understand what drives release of information (ROI) turnaround from the point of view of a specialist working the queue, how it differs by who is asking, and whether the suggest-only position on redaction assistance from July 2025 still holds when a specialist thinks about volume. The participant is an HIM specialist doing ROI fulfillment. They have the same role label as P69 (July 2025 contextual inquiry) and P124 (June 2026 interview), but they are a different person from both and took part in neither session.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The AI coding-suggestion tool is still a concept from July 2025 and has not been built or piloted. No ROI redaction tool has been built or piloted either. 42 CFR Part 2 handling is still fully manual. The participant does intake and first-pass review and redaction on ROI requests, and passes anything that needs Part 2 handling to the second-level review the March 2026 supervisor described. They have released records containing scribe-drafted notes since GA.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(redaction)* They hold the suggest-only position: a person redacts and a person releases, with no auto-release. Within that, they would accept a tool that pre-highlighted possible redactions across a batch of requests at once, but only for low-risk request types. Their example was a patient asking for their own visit notes or test results, where nothing is going to a third party. Each record would still be opened, checked and released by a specialist. They would not want batch pre-highlighting on attorney or insurer requests, or anything that might involve Part 2, and said those should stay one at a time.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(turnaround)* Turnaround depends mostly on who is asking, not on how long the record is. By their own informal estimate, a patient request for their own recent records is usually out "within a few days". Insurer requests take about a week, because they need checking against the authorization and are often for long date ranges. Attorney requests are the slowest, "often two weeks or more", because of authorization checks, broad "complete record" scopes, and a higher chance of Part 2 content going to second-level review. These are their impressions, not counts. They fit the March 2026 supervisor's point that the backlog sits with Part 2 review, and add that attorney requests feed that queue more than other types.
- **[LOW]** *(portal)* Since patients could download more of their own records through the patient portal, they've noticed fewer simple patient requests for recent visit notes and results coming to ROI, though they couldn't say how many. What's left of patient requests tends to be older records or things the portal doesn't show. They didn't think it had made a noticeable difference to the overall backlog, which is driven by third-party requests.

## Representative Quotes
> "For a patient asking for their own notes, sure, run the whole batch and highlight what I should look at. I'll still open every one and I'll still be the one who hits send. Attorney requests, no. Those I do one at a time."
> — HIM Specialist (Release of Information), P128

> "It's not how thick the chart is, it's who's asking. A patient's done in a few days. An insurer's about a week. An attorney? Two weeks if I'm lucky, because there's always something that has to go up for the second look."
> — HIM Specialist (Release of Information), P128

## Recommendations
1. If ROI redaction assistance is scoped, keep it suggest-only with a human releasing each record. Treat batch pre-highlighting for low-risk, patient-directed request types as a possible first scope, and exclude third-party and possible Part 2 requests from batch handling.
2. Report ROI turnaround by requester type (patient, insurer, attorney, other) rather than as one figure, so the effect of any change can be seen where the backlog actually is.
3. Check how much patient request volume the portal has absorbed before using overall request volume to size any tool.
