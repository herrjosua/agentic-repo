# Interview — Complex Care Coordinator on Alert Volume

## Objective
Understand where alert noise comes from for a coordinator on a high-risk panel, and whether AI ranking addresses it, following up the August 2025 alert-triage test and the May 2026 supervisor's estimate that many alerts are administrative rather than clinical.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a care coordinator on a complex care team with a high-risk panel. Their team works the chronological queue; the AI-ranked queue hasn't been built. They reacted to a description of the ranked queue, not to screens. They did not take part in any earlier session.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(repeat alerts)* For them, the main source of noise is repeat alerts for the same patient, more than the order the alerts come in. They would want alerts grouped by patient before any ranking is applied, so that they deal with one patient once rather than meeting them several times down the queue. This is their account; no queue data was reviewed in this session.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(patient history)* Their judgement of a patient's risk leans on knowing that patient's history, which a ranking based on the alert alone wouldn't have. This is a second voice alongside P81's 2025 comment about knowing a patient's history, from a different coordinator on a different kind of panel.

## Representative Quotes
> "It's not the order that gets me, it's seeing the same patient four times. Put them together first, then rank them if you want."
> — Care Coordinator (complex care), P134

> "I know these patients. Half of deciding who's at risk is knowing what happened to them last winter."
> — Care Coordinator (complex care), P134

## Recommendations
1. Explore grouping alerts by patient as a step before ranking, and test it with coordinators on high-risk panels.
2. Keep patient history in view when ranking signals are next revisited, in line with the August 2025 recommendation.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How many alerts are repeats for the same patient is not measured; this is one coordinator's account.
- How the 2025 ranking was built and how accurate it is are still not documented, and whether the override-capture idea reached the data-science team isn't recorded.
- Real-world triage speed and how often coordinators would follow a ranked order without judgement are not measured.
