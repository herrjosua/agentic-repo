# Interview — ICU Nurse on the Handoff Narrative

## Objective
The February 2025 nursing survey's top volunteered concern was losing the clinical narrative voice, and an ICU respondent wrote that their notes are how they hand off a patient safely. This session asked an ICU nurse what the narrative in their notes does at shift handoff, and what they would expect of AI drafting nursing notes, which doesn't exist.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a senior registered nurse in an adult ICU. Their charting is manual; the ambient scribe, GA since January 2026, drafts notes for physicians, NPs and PAs only, and no nurse uses it for their own documentation. Everything they said about AI drafting nursing notes is an expectation, not experience. They did not take part in any earlier session; they are not P07, the ICU nurse quoted in the February 2025 survey.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(handoff narrative)* The narrative part of their note is a safety artifact: it is where they record what worried them and why, the trend they are watching, and what they would do if it got worse. Flowsheet values show what happened; the narrative shows what the next nurse should watch for. At handoff the oncoming nurse reads it before the verbal report, and they described it as the one part of the note they write for a specific reader.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(expectation of AI drafting)* They were skeptical of any AI drafting nursing notes. Their expectation was that a draft could summarise the flowsheet but would not know what they were worried about, and that a nurse at the end of a twelve-hour shift would accept it rather than rewrite it. They saw that as worse than writing less. This is an expectation of a tool that doesn't exist.
- **[LOW]** *(where help might fit)* They could see help with the parts that repeat flowsheet data in prose, as long as the narrative stayed theirs. This came up once, unprompted, and wasn't explored.

## Representative Quotes
> "The numbers are in the flowsheet. My note is what I was worried about at three a.m. That's what the next nurse needs."
> — Registered Nurse (ICU), P140

> "At hour twelve, if it hands me a paragraph, I'm going to sign it. That's what scares me."
> — Registered Nurse (ICU), P140

## Recommendations
1. Treat the nursing narrative as a handoff safety artifact in any future discussion of AI drafting for nurses, not only as a documentation-burden item.
2. If AI drafting for nursing notes is ever scoped, test end-of-shift acceptance of drafts directly, since this nurse's concern was about tired nurses accepting a draft, not about accuracy.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- This is one ICU nurse. It is consistent with the February 2025 survey's note that ICU and ED nurses were more skeptical, but one interview does not validate that unit-level claim, which was low-confidence and had no per-unit figures.
- Whether skepticism varies with years of experience (the survey's tenure question) is still open; this interview can't answer it.
- How nurses on other units use the narrative at handoff is not known.
