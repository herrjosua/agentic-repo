# Interview — ED Charge Nurse on Shift Oversight

## Objective
The ED intake boundary in `scope-boundaries-and-workflow-fit.md` rests on one intake nurse during peak hours (2025-04-22). This session asked a charge nurse, who oversees the whole department across a shift, whether that boundary describes the ED as a whole or only intake at peak, and where, if anywhere, they could see an AI assist fitting in their own work.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The participant is a senior ED nurse who works as charge nurse on evening shifts: bed assignments, staffing, flow, and the end-of-shift handoff to the next charge nurse. Nurse charting in the ED is manual; the ambient scribe drafts notes for physicians, NPs and PAs only. They did not take part in any earlier session. They are not P21, the ED intake nurse from the April 2025 shadowing, and not P97, the behavioral health unit charge nurse from October 2025.

## Key Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** *(peak vs off-peak)* They split the shift into peak, when nothing that needs attention can be added, and the hours after it, when there is time to catch up. They agreed with the intake boundary for peak and said it holds for charge work at peak too, but did not think it describes the whole shift. This refines the ED boundary: it covers intake during peak, not the ED at every hour.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(after the shift)* Where they could see an assist fitting is after peak: pulling together the end-of-shift handoff (who is boarding, who is waiting on a bed, what is still pending) from what is already in the chart, for them to check and correct. They put assembling it by hand at "twenty minutes on a bad night"; that is an informal estimate, not timed. This is an expectation of something that doesn't exist; nothing was shown in this session.
- **[LOW]** *(documentation)* Catching up on their own charting after peak is routine for the ED nurses they supervise. They did not think AI drafting of nursing notes would help at peak, for the same reason as intake: nobody could review it then.

## Representative Quotes
> "At seven o'clock, no, nothing new, I agree with your intake nurse. At one in the morning I've got time to look at something."
> — Charge Nurse (emergency department), P139

> "If it could pull together who's boarding and what's still pending, and I fix what's wrong, that's my handoff. That I'd look at."
> — Charge Nurse (emergency department), P139

## Recommendations
1. State the ED boundary as peak-specific (intake, and charge work, during peak) rather than ED-wide, so it isn't read as ruling out off-peak work.
2. If ED work is revisited, treat an end-of-shift handoff summary for the charge nurse as a candidate to explore, separately from intake.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How long assembling the charge handoff takes is not measured; "twenty minutes on a bad night" is one charge nurse's estimate.
- Whether day-shift charge nurses, or other EDs, split the shift the same way is not known; this is one interview.
- What causes the system slowness the April 2025 session reported is still open; it was not discussed here.
