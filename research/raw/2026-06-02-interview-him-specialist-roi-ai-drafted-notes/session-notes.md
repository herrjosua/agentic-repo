# Interview — HIM Specialist on Releasing AI-Drafted Notes

## Objective
Understand how releasing records that contain scribe-drafted notes has changed day-to-day release of information (ROI) work since the ambient scribe went GA in January 2026: what the specialist thinks counts as the released legal record, what new redaction work the scribe has created, and what they would still expect from a redaction-assistance tool. The participant is an HIM specialist doing ROI fulfillment. They have the same role label as P69 in the July 2025 contextual inquiry but are a different person, and did not take part in that session.

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** The AI coding-suggestion tool is still a concept from July 2025 and has not been built or piloted; no ROI redaction tool has been built or piloted either. 42 CFR Part 2 handling is still fully manual. The participant does first-pass review and redaction on ROI requests and passes anything that needs Part 2 handling to the second-level review described in the March 2026 supervisor interview.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(legal record)* The specialist isn't sure what the released legal record is for a scribe-drafted note. They release the signed note as it appears in the chart, but they've heard the scribe keeps generation metadata somewhere (a draft, a transcript, or both, they weren't sure) and don't know whether that is part of the designated record set, whether a requester or an attorney could ask for it, or who would decide. Nobody has given ROI guidance on it since GA. They have had one request from an attorney's office that asked for "all versions and drafts" of a visit note; it was escalated to the supervisor and they didn't hear how it was resolved.
- **[HIGH]** *(sensitive notes)* The scribe captures things said in passing that a physician typing their own note would usually have left out, for example a patient mentioning a family member's diagnosis or a relative's substance use, or a side comment about a partner. These end up in the signed note and have to be found and redacted before release, page by page, because they're not in a predictable section. By their own informal estimate, they find something like this in "maybe one in five or six" scribe-drafted notes in a typical release, though they said that was a guess and depends heavily on the clinic. This is separate from Part 2: most of what they redact here isn't Part 2 content, but some of it could be, which pushes the request into second-level review.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(trust)* Asked about redaction assistance, they reaffirmed the July 2025 position: highlighting what might need redaction would help, especially for the incidental content above, but a person has to make the redaction and do the final release. They'd want to see why something was flagged. They were more skeptical than they expected to be about the tool missing things: "it's the stuff it doesn't flag that I'd lose sleep over."

## Representative Quotes
> "When somebody asks for the record, I send the signed note. But if there's a draft sitting behind it that the scribe made, is that the record too? I genuinely don't know, and I don't think anybody's told us."
> — HIM Specialist (Release of Information), P124

> "The scribe writes down everything. Mom's diagnosis, the brother's drinking, whatever came up while they were talking. Now that's in the chart, and I'm the one who has to catch it before it goes out the door."
> — HIM Specialist (Release of Information), P124

## Recommendations
1. Ask Legal/Compliance to state whether scribe generation metadata (drafts, transcripts, provenance) is part of the designated record set or legal record, and give ROI written guidance. Add this to the still-pending ratification of the CMIO's August 2025 attestation model.
2. Share examples of incidental third-party and sensitive content captured by the scribe with the ambient scribe team, as input to what the drafting step leaves out of the note.
3. If ROI redaction assistance is scoped, keep it suggest-only with a human doing the redaction and final release, show why each item was flagged, and test specifically for missed incidental content, not only Part 2 content.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- How was the "all versions and drafts" attorney request resolved, and by whom?
- Does the scribe retain a transcript or draft after the note is signed, and for how long?
