# Interview — HIM Supervisor on ROI Volume and Part 2 Handling

## Objective
Revisit release of information (ROI) eight months after the July 2025 contextual inquiry: how request volume and turnaround stand now, whether anything has changed in how 42 CFR Part 2 records are handled, and whether the ambient scribe going GA in January 2026 has changed the work. The participant is a different HIM supervisor from the one in the July 2025 session (P67).

## Method
- **Method:** 1:1 interview, 45 min
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder
- **Context:** Participant supervises ROI request intake and fulfillment and does second-level Part 2 review themselves. Part 2 handling is still fully manual; no ROI redaction tool has been built or piloted.

## Key Findings
- **[HIGH]** *(42 CFR Part 2)* Part 2 records are still identified from memory. There is still no structured flag in the EHR, and nothing has changed since July 2025. Newer specialists work from a printed sheet of the clinics and note types that "usually" need Part 2 handling and ask a senior specialist when unsure. Because nothing says which notes actually come from a Part 2 program, staff send any substance-use mention to the stricter review to be safe, wherever the note came from. The supervisor said scribe-drafted notes from general medical visits sometimes capture that part of the conversation in more detail than a physician would have typed, so specialists now read those notes more closely.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(volume)* By their own informal count, ROI requests are up "somewhere around a quarter" on a year ago. That differs from the ~18% year-over-year figure the other supervisor gave in July 2025. This supervisor counts every request received, re-requests and follow-ups from requesters chasing a late response included, which may explain part of the gap. Both figures are informal and neither has been checked against system data.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(turnaround)* The backlog is concentrated in requests that need Part 2 review, not routine ones. They named four drivers: only a few senior specialists can do the second-level Part 2 review; released records are longer because scribe-drafted notes are longer, so page-by-page review takes more time; re-requests add intake work for requests already in the queue; and one open specialist position. By their own informal count, turnaround on requests that need Part 2 review has slipped from about a week to closer to two.

## Representative Quotes
> "Part 2 is still tribal knowledge. It's in my head, it's in the heads of two or three people on my team, and it's on a sheet taped up by the printer. That's the system."
> — HIM Supervisor, P121

> "The easy requests go out fine. What's stuck is anything that needs the second look for Part 2, because there's only a couple of us who can do it, and everything just piles up behind us."
> — HIM Supervisor, P121

## Recommendations
1. Re-escalate the structured Part 2 flag as a standalone platform gap. It has now come up in three separate sessions (HIM in July 2025, behavioral health in October 2025, and here), and nothing has changed.
2. If ROI redaction assistance is scoped later, treat the second-level Part 2 review queue as the bottleneck to design around, not first-pass redaction.
3. Pull system-of-record ROI volume and turnaround data. This was an open follow-up in July 2025 and is still outstanding.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- Reconcile the two informal volume estimates (~18% in July 2025, roughly a quarter here) against actual request counts, and agree on whether re-requests count as requests.
- Check whether pages per released record can be measured before and after GA, to test the "notes are longer now" impression.
